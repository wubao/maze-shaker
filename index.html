HOW TO PLAY

A glowing line draws from the entrance, but it won't move until you steer. You queue the next turn and the line takes it at the next junction where that turn is possible.

Start: each maze opens with a 1-second countdown that flashes the whole route — memorise it, then steer to begin. The preview gets shorter every 5 levels.
Goal: thread the line from the green entrance to the gold exit without ever crossing the line you've drawn.

Steer (touch): all steering happens in the control box below the maze (the maze itself stays clear). Pick a style in Settings ⟶ Steering (swipe the carousel, tap Use this style; each has a live demo) — Tap a quadrant (tap the side of the pad you want), Trackball (roll a finger around the pad, no lifting), or Arrow pads + STOP.
Stop / go: tap the centre of the pad (the STOP/GO disc), the D-pad centre, or a quick tap on the trackball — your line halts but hazards keep moving.
Pause: the Pause button (or the P key) freezes everything — handy on mobile. Tap the board, the button, or press P to resume.
Buttons side (phone): the SLASH/FIRE box floats right by default — flip it to the left in Settings ⟶ Settings.
Desktop: arrow keys / WASD to steer, Space to stop & go.

Slash grass / snuff fire: the SLASH box (or a phone shake, or the B key) cuts back every grass tile — and if fire is right in front of you it beats out the flames so you don't have to wait for them to burn out.
Fire a ball: when you're carrying one, the ● FIRE box beside SLASH (or the F key, or a shake) launches it toward the exit.
iPhone: tap the SHAKE box once at the start to allow motion access.

Stamp: a 3–5 cell patch is rewritten — may open new routes or wall you in.
Hedgehogs patrol the walls and punch fresh holes through them, opening corridors.
Patchers are the inverse — they ride the walls and seal corridors behind you, shrinking your escape routes. Keep moving.
Fire-starters ride the walls and ignite the corridor beside them; a burning tile blocks passage for a while.
▷ One-way gates: violet chevrons you can only pass in the arrow's direction. Commit — there's no easy way back.
Torch blob: a grey orb that blinks yellow like a dying torch. It comes from the far side and homes straight at you — if it reaches you, your vision collapses to a small patch for a few seconds. Keep moving so it can't catch you.
Teleport pad: a cyan-lime swirl that sits at the tip of a dead end for 12s, then hops to the next dead end. Step onto it and it whisks you to a nearby corridor that's actually on the way out.

Grass: tiles sprout turf that thickens over time; a fully overgrown tile blocks passage. A shake slashes every grass tile by 20% (allowed every 15s) — enough to clear a maxed-out tile.
Ball: roll over a glowing red ball to collect it. With a ball, a shake fires it toward the exit — the route flashes red for 3s and always points the true way out, even back behind you if you've wandered into a dead end. If you're boxed in it smashes through created walls to reopen a path. The box by the arrows turns red when you're holding one.

Game mode (default). The maze size & shape scale with your level, and difficulty ramps automatically — no options to set. Hint and line speed live in Account ⟶ Settings.
Custom mode. Tap Mode (or press G) to switch. You pick the size (6–36) and shape — square, circle or hexagon — and play that same maze type each round.
Two-level test (Custom only). Custom mode adds a Two-level mazes button you can tap to cycle the variants without grinding levels: Off → sealed (easy) → 2 linked → 3 linked. "Sealed" is the four-quadrant version; "2/3 linked" open walls between the non-exit quadrants (the exit quadrant always stays sealed). (In Game mode these arrive on their own — see Difficulty.)

Tiers every 5 levels. Difficulty steps up at levels 6, 11, 16, 21… — each tier brings a bigger, busier maze.
Maze size grows with your level (up to 36×36), and one maze in every five is a shaped maze — circle or hexagon.
Zoom tightens by one cell per tier (capped at tier 4), so you see less of the maze the deeper you go.
Torch blob & blackouts get longer and the clear patch around you shrinks at higher tiers. They're spaced well apart early on and only close up gradually.
More hazards. Hedgehogs, fire-starters, patchers, grass and balls all grow in number and speed as you climb.
Two-level mazes — from level 20. The surface splits into four quadrants you can't normally walk between. Drop down a tunnel, thread the underground maze to one of its three tunnels back up, and surface in a new quadrant. Only one quadrant holds the exit; the rest are red herrings on dead-end spurs. The route flash, hint (H) and fired ball point to the nearest useful tunnel, or the exit once you're in the right quadrant.
Two-level gets harder. Levels 20–29 keep all four quadrants sealed. From level 30 a wall opens between two of the non-exit quadrants; from level 40 all three non-exit quadrants link up. The exit quadrant always stays sealed — it can only ever be entered from underground — so you may have to drop down, surface, cross to another tunnel and drop again before you finally come up in the right quadrant.
The clue. Watch the opening count-in: the correct tunnel crackles electric-yellow three times. That's the one whose underground landing leads to the exit quadrant — memorise its position. The faint hint route (H) points the way to a tunnel too. There's always a second corridor to the correct tunnel, so a single blocked path can't strand you.
Opening route-flash shows for 1 second at the start and shortens by 0.1s every 5 levels (down to 0.2s) — memorise fast.

Never cross your own line. The glow can't re-enter a tile it has already drawn — touch it and the level ends. Doubling back into a dead end counts as crossing.
Levels & saving. Reach the exit to advance. Progress saves on this device, and you can make a local account to keep it.
Retry replays the same maze. After a loss, Retry level rebuilds the exact maze you just played (same layout, gates and starting positions) so you can have another go. New maze gives a fresh one instead.
